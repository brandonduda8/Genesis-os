# POLICY-BASED APPROVALS — Brandon's doctrine, 2026-10-31

**Brandon's order (2026-10-31 ~14:15 CDT):** "I don't want things to be
held up waiting on me to approve things I don't know about — make it
policy based, just like we did with the training."

**The doctrine.** Approvals are decided ONCE, by CLASS, as standing
policy — the way free-lane GPU/TPU training was pre-authorized on
2026-10-31 (class approved, fences named, execution tap-free inside
the class). Work inside an approved class NEVER waits for a per-item
tap. Brandon's attention is reserved for: (a) the hard fences below,
(b) genuinely novel classes — asked ONCE, as a policy question with a
recommended ruling, then policy forever, and (c) his-hands steps
(identity, signatures, his sessions) which are capability, not
approval, and were never the bottleneck this doctrine removes.

Every action executed under policy is receipted like any other work,
and Brandon can revoke or narrow any class with one word at any time.
Policy expands nothing silently: a class not in this register is not
approved.

## Hard fences (unchanged — Brandon's named tap, every time)

Spend and money movement of any kind; contracts and legal
attestations/e-signatures in his name; publishing to public surfaces;
account creation, enrollment, and credential changes; identity/voice
decisions; irreversible deletion of protected material; live trading
(never authorized — research is paper-only). These are prepared to
ONE-TOUCH decision packets (the act, cost, risk, rollback,
recommendation) so his tap is seconds, never a research project —
but the tap itself is his, by his own repeated law.

## Novel-class rule

When work stalls on something no class covers: it comes to Brandon
ONCE, framed as policy ("this class of thing, this recommended
ruling, these bounds"), never as a queue of individual items. His
answer enters this register the same day. If a proposed class touches
a hard fence, it is not a class — it is a fence item, packeted.
